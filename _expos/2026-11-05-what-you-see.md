 First stage. Streets are starting to empty. No one can know if it is linked to the recent events, or to some more discreet deflagration, that takes place in homes. A secret rumor that gain volume and amplitude. We wouldn't say that everyone keeps talking about it ; still, it is a white noise that gets noticed, clearing a wide field, pass pavement, seeping throughs certains facades.

Perhaps everything started at Saint-Gilles, early one evening, leafing some artists books and others rares editions displayed on some coffee table of a living room. Looking for a mythical artist (keep away gossips but), who might have send out his humble metropolitan dealer because now too much comfortable in the coolness. This and other things.

It’s not easy to unravel fact from fiction, it’s interchangeable, modulables in a lot of others timelines (let’s not spoil out our pleasure). It’s a reflection of time. We keep it on mind, we assume. Clearly, clearly. Supposedly for the love of the surface. I got nothing to tell. Like Gladys Clover, there is numerous days where I would like to simply not open my mouth, neither being visible from someone else. One of these days, I would like to go to a restaurant wearing sunglasses. I would look at the wall for thinking ; if only the wall as the world let me alone, everything will be easier.

But it’s half true. Even the act of disappearing evoked a desire to be seen. Both present and absent, he could murmur three words in a corner and at the end no one would forget. And while I establish my appearance as a silhouette I told myself that for Gladys Clover, to no be here was a an an efficient solution. I am empty and I like this superficiality. When people go so far as to say that texts, after all, merely guide the recipient's gaze in turn, as do the operators of vision in the art that the work is meant to criticize, they are wrong, of course.

These operators usually serve to make others see, not to be seen. They simply belong to that operation.

*This practice of not produce neither realize press release stemmed, in Filippo di Manzone’s explanation, from the stance that there was a very small window of time in which the visual work of art is experienced before this perception is enviably shaped by language, and this window should be kept open as long as possible ; that we should resist prematurely foreclosing the meaning derived from the visual, resisting as long as possible channelling the viewer’s experience through the strictures of language.*
